How We Work / implementation lifecycle

Make the path from problem to implementation clear.

A structured approach for business owners and teams: understand what is happening, design a practical change, implement it carefully, and leave people prepared to run it.

Six connected stages

Clarity at each handoff.

Scope and pace depend on the project. These stages provide a shared way to understand decisions and keep the work grounded in operational needs.

01 / DISCOVERY & ASSESSMENT

Understand the current operation

Talk through goals, review how work moves today, and identify the tools, people, constraints, and points of friction involved.

What becomes clearer: the problem, scope, and questions to resolve.
02 / SOLUTION DESIGN

Agree what should change

Shape a future workflow, clarify which systems are involved, decide what information needs to move, and agree where people need to review or decide.

What becomes clearer: proposed workflow, system roles, and boundaries.
03 / IMPLEMENTATION & CONFIGURATION

Build and configure the agreed approach

Set up systems and workflows, configure suitable integrations, and prepare agreed changes in line with the design.

What takes shape: the configured system and documented changes.
04 / TESTING & VALIDATION

Walk through real situations

Review typical workflows and exceptions with the people involved. Confirm that information, permissions, and handoffs behave as intended.

What is checked: realistic scenarios and exception paths.
05 / TRAINING & HANDOVER

Prepare the team to use it

Explain what has changed, where to find guidance, who owns key tasks, and how to raise or handle common issues.

What is handed over: useful guidance, ownership, and next steps.
06 / ONGOING IMPROVEMENT

Keep the system useful

Review feedback and operational changes, address new friction, and refine the approach when priorities or workflows evolve.

What continues: considered improvement based on the work.

Plain language, visible decisions

Technical complexity should not hide the next step.

At each stage, the practical questions stay visible: what problem are we solving, who relies on this process, what changes for them, and how will the team know where to go when something is different?

A

People know what is changing

Scope, responsibilities, and handoffs are described in terms of the work—not just the software.

B

Exceptions are considered

Testing includes what happens when information is missing, an approval is needed, or a step cannot proceed.

C

Ownership does not disappear at launch

Handover includes the basic guidance and decision routes needed to support day-to-day use.

Questions business owners often ask

Start with the operation you have.

Do we need to know which software to use before a conversation?

No. It is useful to start with the work, friction, or information gaps you want to address. The right tools depend on the operational needs and constraints.

Can the work focus on a process before a new system is introduced?

Yes. Process clarity can be an important first step, whether or not a platform change or integration follows.

How are proposed outcomes handled?

Possible operational outcomes are discussed as design intentions, not guaranteed results. They depend on the agreed scope, current data, the selected systems, and how changes are adopted.
